Submit a spectrum from the [submit page](/submit-data/).
You must [sign in](/docs/share/sign-in/) first.
A moderator checks each submission before EELSdb publishes it.

You need a [confirmed email address](#confirmed-email-address) to submit. EELSdb may also ask for a linked [ORCID iD](/docs/share/sign-in/#link-orcid-google-or-github) (see [ORCID iD](#orcid-id)).

Before you start, have these ready:

- the spectrum file (see [File formats](/docs/share/file-formats/));
- the chemical formula and the name of the specimen;
- the beam energy, the convergence semi-angle and the collection semi-angle, if you know them (see [Beam energy and angles](/docs/share/beam-energy-and-angles/));
- the DOI of the paper that uses the spectrum, if there is one.

The form has a step for each group of fields. The form marks the required fields.
You can go back to an earlier step at any time from the list of steps.

## Fill in the form

<Steps>

1. **Files.** Drop the spectrum file on the form, or click **choose files**.

   Your browser then reads the file. The form shows a plot of the spectrum, runs the [file checks](/docs/share/checks/), and fills in the fields that it can read from the file.
   The plot stays beside the form for the next steps.
   If your browser cannot read the file, you can still fill in the form and submit.

   You can add more files for the same spectrum. A different specimen needs its own submission.
   Under the plot of each file, choose what the file is in **This file is**:

   - **Raw data** or **Background subtracted**: the spectrum. If you send both, the background-subtracted file is the main spectrum, and EELSdb keeps the raw data with it.
   - **Processed**: a processed version of the same spectrum, for example oscillator strength or smoothed (see [Processed versions](/docs/share/file-formats/#processed-versions)).
   - **Companion low-loss (for thickness)**: EELSdb uses it to estimate the specimen thickness.
   - **Companion file (beam settings)**: a file from the same session, for example a Velox `.emd` or TIA `.ser` survey. These files often record the beam energy and the convergence angle.

   The form makes a first choice for each file. Check it.

2. **Specimen.** Type the specimen name and the chemical formula, for example `Fe2O3` or `(La0.7,Sr0.3)MnO3`, and choose the spectrum type. **X-ray absorption (NEXAFS)** is for near-edge X-ray absorption spectra of core edges, up to about 3 keV.

   The form fills in **Elements** from the formula. Correct the list if it is wrong: type a symbol or a name to add an element, and click an element to remove it.

   **Edges** lists the edges in the spectrum. EELSdb adds the edges it finds in your spectrum, and the main edges of your elements in the energy range of your spectrum. The edges also show on the plot and in the list under it. Remove any that are wrong.
   To add an edge, type its element in **Edges** and choose the edge, or click the plot at its energy. You can add minor edges in the same way.

   Also fill in the phase (solid, liquid or gas), the intensity units, the source and purity of the specimen, keywords and comments.

3. **X-ray absorption** (only for X-ray absorption spectra). Type the facility, the beamline, the detection mode and the other beamline settings. An XDI file fills in some of these fields.

4. **Acquisition.** Choose an instrument profile if your microscope has one. A profile fills in calibrated angles.

   Then give the microscope, the detector, the gun type and the acquisition mode.
   Choose the beam energy (60, 80, 120, 200 or 300 kV, **Other** or **Unknown**), and type the convergence and collection semi-angles in mrad.
   Mark a value **Approximate** and give an uncertainty, or mark it **Unknown**. Both are valid answers.

5. **Treatment.** Tell readers how you processed the data: background subtraction, dark current and gain corrections, the energy calibration, the thickness in t/λ, and any deconvolution. The form fills in **Background subtracted** from your choice for the spectrum file.

6. **Reference.** Paste the DOI of the paper. The form looks it up on CrossRef and fills in the authors, the title and the journal.
   If there is no DOI, give a web link or type the reference.
   Under **Associated EELSdb spectra**, paste the addresses of related spectra on EELSdb, for example the low-loss spectrum of the same specimen.

7. **Review.** Read the summary and the results of the file checks.
   Agree to publish the data under the [Open Database Licence (ODbL)](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/), then click **Submit for review**.
   Anyone may then reuse your data, also commercially, if they credit you and the EELS Data Base. You cannot withdraw the licence later.
   If you cannot publish under the ODbL, [contact the administrators](/contact/) before you submit. They can agree another open licence with you, such as CC BY 4.0, and the form then asks you to agree to that one.

</Steps>

If a field has a problem, the form shows it in red after you click **Submit for review**.
EELSdb does not keep the files after an error, so attach them again before you submit again.

## Confirmed email address

Moderators email you when they publish your spectrum, ask for changes or turn it down, so you need a confirmed email address to submit. Without one, the submit page shows **Confirm your email address first** in place of the form. If you gave an address, click **Send the confirmation email**. If you did not, type your address and click the same button. Then open the link in the email in the same browser, while you are signed in, and reload the submit page. The same applies when you submit [drafts](/docs/share/drafts/).

## ORCID iD

An administrator can require a linked ORCID iD to submit spectra. While the rule is on, the submit page tells you if you need one and shows a **Link ORCID** button. Click it, approve the request at ORCID, and you return to the page. The same applies when you submit [drafts](/docs/share/drafts/). Editing a spectrum that is already published, and making drafts with an assistant, do not need an ORCID iD.

## After you submit

Your [submissions page](/account/submissions/) lists each submission and its status:

- Waiting for review: A moderator has not decided yet.
- Changes requested: A moderator asks you to change something. The notes column and an email tell you what.
- Published: The spectrum is public.
- Not accepted: A moderator rejected the submission. The notes tell you why.

When a moderator requests changes, edit the submission and save it. It then goes back into the review queue.

At review, the moderator's browser reads your file again with a pinned version of RosettaSciIO and runs the checks again.
When they approve it, EELSdb publishes the spectrum with your original file, an MSA file with the EELS header fields, and a HyperSpy (`.hspy`) file with the metadata.

To submit many spectra, let an assistant or a script [prepare them as drafts](/docs/share/drafts/).
